1. What the site does

MT Alchemy's tagline is "We don't just run activities. We shift mindsets, behaviours, and team culture." The platform turns visitors into enquiries, learners and corporate clients through four connected modules:

Corporate website

A single landing page with sections for About, Services, Upskilling, Store, Insights and Contact. Each section links to a full page. There's also an Impact Stories carousel, a free EQ self-assessment and a "For organisations" enquiry form.

Learning management system

A course catalogue filtered by each learner's pathway, with purchase, enrolment and a modular course player (video, PDF, reading and quiz lessons). Progress is tracked, and completing a course issues a certificate with a public verification link.

E-commerce store

Digital products are downloaded after payment. Physical products are shipped and tracked from Processing to Shipped to Delivered. One checkout handles products, courses and employer seats. Payments run in test mode.

Role-based portals

Learners: learning, catalogue, certificates, orders, downloads and profile. Employers: employees, seats, assignments, progress and analytics charts. Trainers: course authoring. Super admins: users, products, orders and shipping, enquiries and content.

2. System architecture

The app is a single Flutter codebase that talks directly to Firebase. There is no custom server: business rules run in the client stores, and Firestore security rules enforce who can read or write each document.

How data flows

  1. AuthStore listens to Firebase Auth. On sign-in it streams users/{uid} (role, organisation, pathway) and the user's organisation.
  2. The other stores listen to AuthStore. When the uid changes, they cancel their old listeners and subscribe to that user's data, for example enrolments where userId == uid.
  3. Screens watch stores and rebuild automatically. CatalogStore starts with the bundled catalogue, so pages draw instantly and work offline. Firestore then replaces it live.
  4. Actions such as checkout, marking a lesson complete or assigning a course write batches or transactions. Progress updates optimistically, so the UI responds before the server confirms.

4. Navigation, landing page and role guards

Public pages share AppShell (one Scaffold with a glass AppBar and a drawer below 1024px). Signed-in pages share PortalShell (a second Scaffold with its own AppBar, a side rail or drawer, a notification bell, and one scroll area with a visible scrollbar).

Landing behaviour: on the home page, the menu items glide to the matching section of the landing page (HomeSections.navigate with Scrollable.ensureVisible). From any other page, they open the full page with a fade-and-lift transition. Each landing section also has a "See all" link.

6. Security model

firestore.rules reads the caller's role from users/{uid}. Key guarantees, each proven by verify.mjs against the live project:

  • Users cannot change their own role or disabled flag. Self-registration can only create individual or employer accounts.
  • An enrolment needs either an order owned by the learner or an employer assignment for their email address.
  • A certificate can only be created when the matching enrolment's status is complete.
  • Employers can only read and write their own organisation's employees, assignments, enrolments and orders.
  • Draft (unpublished) courses are hidden from everyone except trainers and super admins.
  • Buyers can create a shipment in the Processing state, but only a super admin can mark it Shipped or Delivered.

7. The five user journeys

A · Individual learning

  1. Register and choose a career stage and interest areas
  2. The course catalogue shows only pathway courses
  3. Buy the course, then pay at checkout (test card 4242…)
  4. Enrolment is created and the dashboard shows progress
  5. Watch the video, read the PDF, Mark complete, Next
  6. Pass the quiz (70% or more) to receive a verifiable certificate

B · Digital product

  1. Store, then choose the EI Self-Coaching Toolkit
  2. Add to cart, then Checkout (no shipping charge)
  3. Payment record is created in test mode
  4. My digital products, then Download (the count is tracked)

C · Physical product

  1. Store, then Cart (free delivery from £50)
  2. Enter a shipping address at checkout and pay
  3. Stock is decremented in a transaction
  4. The admin marks it Shipped (with tracking), then Delivered, and the buyer is notified each time

D · Employer

  1. Register an organisation
  2. Add employees (they join automatically when they register with that email)
  3. Buy courses: choose the number of seats and pay
  4. Assign courses, which uses up the seats
  5. Progress report and analytics charts

E · Corporate enquiry

  1. Open "For organisations" from the landing page or footer
  2. Submit an enquiry (saved to Firestore)
  3. The super admin inbox tracks it: New, In discussion, Proposal, Agreed
  4. The admin writes the customised programme proposal

Extras

An EQ self-assessment with an animated profile and a recommended course, an Impact Stories carousel, reviews, notifications, dark mode, live admin totals, and public certificate verification.

8. Learning pathways and course eligibility

At registration, a learner picks a career stage (which sets the course levels they can take) and one or more interest areas (which set the topics). A course is open to them only when it matches both. Learners can buy any course in that set. Other courses stay visible but locked, with the reason shown. Employer assignments bypass the rule because the organisation chooses for its staff.

The catalogue holds 12 courses, one for every level in each of the four practice areas, so every combination of stage and interests has at least one course to choose from. The rule lives in lib/data/eligibility.dart, and the catalogue, course page and checkout all use it.

9. Design system, imagery and motion

  • Colour: pure white (#FFFFFF) with a zinc ash scale and Crimson Red (#C8102E) as the single accent. Grey-to-red gradients mark moments of change, such as a passed quiz, a completed order or a finished course. There is no grain or grey haze.
  • Glassmorphism: a shared GlassPanel (backdrop blur, translucent fill, light border) is used on the navigation, hero copy, summaries and forms.
  • Typography: Fraunces for headings and Manrope for body text.
  • Imagery: 30 free-licence Unsplash photos (WebP), one on every course, product, service and article card. Courses and products store an imageUrl that admins can change. The seeded red ink artwork is the fallback.
  • Motion: a looping landing film (Remotion) with a floating glass crystal and parallax particles; staggered reveals; hover lift, red-tinted shadow and photo zoom on every card; 3D tilt on service cards; fade-and-lift page transitions; a smooth glide to landing sections; animated assessment bars. Reduced-motion settings are respected throughout.
  • Responsive: breakpoints at 600px and 1024px. Fluid gutters, layouts that collapse below 1024px, and a full-window canvas (viewport meta, no fixed widths).
